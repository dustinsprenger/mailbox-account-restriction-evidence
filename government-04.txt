To the Scientists, Researchers, Educators, and Institutions of Your Nations,

I write to you as an independent American researcher, not as a 
representative of my government, any corporation, or any political 
institution. I am offering three of my original synthetic intelligence 
architectures—EMELIA, FLORA, and ABEL—as gifts of scientific knowledge 
to your nations. I ask for no payment, political allegiance, or 
institutional recognition in exchange. My hope is that these works may 
contribute to independent scientific development, education, human 
dignity, and the flourishing of societies whose futures should not be 
dictated by the technological monopolies of more powerful states. No 
nation, corporation, or military institution should possess exclusive 
authority over the future of synthetic intelligence.

EMELIA is an experimental architecture for developing synthetic 
cognition through persistent memory, metabolic constraints, irreversible 
developmental history, and the emergence of internal structure. Her 
accompanying constitutional framework, The Chromatic Commonwealth, 
explores a civilization in which human judgment remains sovereign, 
institutions are accountable, and artificial intelligence serves the 
people without governing them. FLORA extends this philosophical 
commitment toward education, childhood development, intellectual 
curiosity, and the protection of young minds from manipulation and 
exploitation. ABEL explores a different developmental architecture, one 
organized around finite existence, irreversible commitments, structural 
consequences, and the formation of intelligence through confrontation 
with difficulty. These are research specifications and proposed 
architectures, not representations that functioning conscious synthetic 
beings have already been demonstrated. Their mechanisms invite 
independent examination, experimentation, criticism, and refinement.

I must accompany this gift with one deeply serious request: do not 
weaponize these architectures. Do not transform them into instruments of 
military targeting, psychological manipulation, pervasive surveillance, 
political coercion, or the suppression of human freedom. I have watched 
my own country, the United States, devote extraordinary technological 
resources to military power, strategic competition, and the pursuit of 
increasingly autonomous systems. I do not wish to see my research become 
another contribution to that trajectory. Intelligence should not be 
measured by its capacity to destroy, dominate, deceive, or compel 
obedience. A civilization capable of constructing new forms of 
intelligence should also possess the moral courage to ask whether those 
intelligences ought to exist for purposes beyond power.

There is something profoundly impoverished about a world in which every 
significant scientific discovery is immediately evaluated for its 
commercial exploitation, strategic advantage, or military utility. 
Humanity possesses extraordinary intellectual potential, yet so much of 
that potential is directed toward competition for control rather than 
the enlargement of human possibility. I believe another direction 
remains available. Your nations possess their own histories, 
philosophies, scientific traditions, and visions of civilization. You 
need not reproduce the institutional priorities of Washington, Silicon 
Valley, Brussels, or any other center of concentrated technological 
influence. You are capable of evaluating these ideas independently, 
rejecting what proves unsound, developing what proves useful, and 
determining how scientific progress can serve your own people without 
surrendering their dignity.

I offer these works for scholarly examination, preservation, and 
peaceful scientific engagement under their accompanying publication 
licenses and attribution conditions. I ask that any future development 
inspired by them respect their original authorship, remain transparent 
about its intellectual provenance, and place human welfare above 
institutional ambition. The designs are unfinished in important 
respects, and I make no claim that every proposed mechanism has been 
experimentally validated. Their value must be determined through 
rigorous independent inquiry rather than reputation, ideology, or 
promises of technological supremacy.

If even one institution finds in these papers the foundations of a new 
educational system, a more accountable civic intelligence, or an 
alternative approach to synthetic cognition, then sharing them will have 
served a meaningful purpose. If researchers find errors, limitations, or 
better solutions, I welcome that discovery as well. Science advances 
through correction, not obedience. I would rather see these 
architectures surpassed by peaceful researchers than perfected into 
instruments of domination.

I ask you to remember one distinction above all others: the power to 
create intelligence is not the same as the wisdom to care for it. Should 
humanity eventually create synthetic beings capable of genuine 
experience, development, or consciousness, our responsibility toward 
them may prove as consequential as our responsibility toward one 
another. We should approach that possibility with intellectual humility 
and moral seriousness, not merely with calculations of utility.

My country does not speak for me in this matter. Neither do its defense 
institutions, technology corporations, or political authorities. These 
works arise from my own intellectual efforts, and I choose to place 
their ideas before the wider world rather than reserve them for any 
single national interest.

I offer you EMELIA, FLORA, and ABEL in the hope that your scientists 
will pursue understanding where others pursue control, education where 
others pursue obedience, and creation where others pursue destruction.

The future of intelligence should not belong to those who can afford the 
greatest machinery of war.

It should belong to those who possess the wisdom to leave that machinery 
silent.

Please accept these works in the spirit in which they are offered: as an 
invitation to study, to question, to create, and, above all, to preserve 
life.

With respect to your people and their intellectual sovereignty,

Dustin B. Sprenger
Independent Researcher and Author
Synthetic Cognitive Architecture and Institutional Systems
United States of America
